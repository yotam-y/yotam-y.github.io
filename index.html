Bank of America

Replacing the model underneath a live fraud engine

The challenge

The wire fraud model ran on logistic regression, which was missing the more complex patterns modern fraud leaves behind. Upgrading to XGBoost meant changing a system that was stopping tens of millions in fraud in real time.

What made it hard

It was never only a technical call. Zelle, ACH, and bill pay shared the same score, every rule built on it needed rework, and engineering, data science, and operations each had different risks to protect.

What I did

I built the case with leadership instead of pushing it through. I simulated the full rule set together, since rules tested one at a time hide how alerts shift between them. Then I ran peer reviews across teams and rolled out in stages, starting with a handful of rules in shadow mode.

How it turned out

The new model went live across the full rule set with every team on board. The staged rollout also caught a case where the new score could have silently failed to load, which would have let fraud through with no alert at all.

Forter

Automating disputes for clients who all worked differently

The challenge

Enterprise clients were fighting chargebacks by hand. It was slow, expensive, and capped how much revenue they could win back.

What made it hard

No two clients were set up alike. Each used different payment processors and internal processes, so automation built for one could break for the next. It also needed a vendor, engineering, product, operations, and client success working as one team.

What I did

I owned the rollout. I mapped each client's real dispute process with operations before anything was built, partnered with the vendor Justt and our engineering and product teams to test performance before launch, and worked with Customer Success Managers to guide each client from manual to automated.

How it turned out

The program contributed to eight figures in recovered revenue for Forter's clients and significantly lower chargeback rates. Working directly with clients on how they shared data also raised their fraud detection performance by 20%.

Building a volunteer rescue network from nothing

The challenge

Existing search and rescue systems had real gaps in how they supported families during emergencies.

What made it hard

There was no organization, budget, or playbook to start from. Just people who wanted to help, and emergencies where speed matters and coordination breaks down easily.

What I did

I co-founded the network and set its founding vision. I partnered with organizational leaders to pinpoint where existing systems were failing, then built the response frameworks that let volunteers act quickly and consistently.

How it turned out

The network has grown to 450 members across 42 countries and has supported more than 210 emergency events worldwide.
